**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

The breakthrough was Phase 0: building the first version on my phone in plain
Claude Online, before I'd opened the agentic repo at all. I already knew the
lack of context and tooling there to be a strength, not a limitation — with
no harness, no spec checks, and no project history to reason about, I could
throw out a half-formed idea, see it rendered, discard it or twist it, and
try again, all in the time it usually takes to write one prompt to an agent.
What I didn't expect was how much that shifted my mood about the whole
assignment: instead of treating it as another checklist item, I found myself
actually having fun exploring what the model could look like, and got
through several genuinely different ideas instead of settling on the first
workable one. That shift — a context-free environment turning out to be as
conducive to fast, playful prototyping as I'd hoped, and then some — was the
real unlock. Everything after it (porting the result into the harness,
making it static, fixing the bugs it surfaced) was execution against
something I already knew was worth building.

**What did this change about who I want to be as a software developer?**

It's made me want to treat "no stakes yet" as a deliberate phase of my
process, rather than something to rush past to get to the real tool. I used
to assume rigour and structure had to come first. Now I want to prototype
loosely before I ever open the harness that holds me to a spec, because the
fun and speed of that first pass is usually where the actual idea comes from
— the structure can come later, once there's something worth structuring.
